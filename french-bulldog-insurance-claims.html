French Bulldog IVDD and BOAS Insurance Claims: How to Document a Mixed Surgery Bill

A practical record checklist for spinal and airway treatment on the same invoice.

What should a mixed IVDD and BOAS claim include?

Start with the complete itemized invoice, the diagnoses recorded by the veterinarian, and a brief explanation of which services treated which condition. Keep clinical records available and submit them through the route your insurer specifies.

For example, Embrace's submission instructions request the diagnosis or reason for the visit and every invoice page, including the full total, taxes and discounts. A payment receipt alone does not describe the work performed. Your own insurer may use a different form or workflow; check the instructions in your account before uploading.

Use a cover sheet that identifies your pet, policy number, hospital, service dates and invoice number. Add the existing claim reference if you are responding to a request for more information. Keep the original invoice intact and put your annotations in a separate document so the reviewer can compare them easily.

Why do spinal and airway procedures need separate explanations?

IVDD involves the discs between vertebrae; ruptured disc material can compress the spinal cord. The American College of Veterinary Surgeons' IVDD guide describes spinal imaging and surgical decompression. BOAS concerns upper airway abnormalities. VCA's explanation of BOAS describes narrowed nostrils, an elongated soft palate and corrective airway procedures.

These are different clinical problems even when they appear on the same hospital invoice. Ask the surgeon to identify the actual spinal diagnosis, location and procedure, alongside the actual airway findings and procedure. Do not substitute a generic phrase such as “Frenchie surgery” for the recorded diagnoses.

For French Bulldog insurance claims, a useful clinical summary explains why each intervention was undertaken. Ask the treating team to describe whether an airway service was treatment of BOAS, management of an anesthesia complication, or another documented problem. That distinction should come from the medical record, rather than an owner's attempt to choose the most favorable claim category.

If a package cannot be broken down, submit the package description and request the insurer's written handling of that bundle.

How do you document shared anesthesia without guessing?

Mark shared services as shared until the hospital can explain them. Ask the insurer what evidence it needs for allocation and ask the clinician whether a defensible breakdown exists. An arbitrary equal split is an owner's assumption, not a medical finding.

VCA notes increased anesthesia complication risk in dogs with BOAS. That clinical risk does not, by itself, establish which condition an insurer should assign an anesthesia charge to. If the team documents additional airway monitoring or a recovery complication, retain that explanation alongside the anesthesia and progress records.

For French Bulldog insurance claims involving shared costs, use neutral wording: “This invoice includes spinal treatment and airway treatment. Please review the attached clinical clarification and explain how shared anesthesia and hospitalization charges were allocated.” Do not ask the clinic to relabel airway surgery as spinal surgery or delete an earlier breathing complaint.

Which dates matter when pre-existing conditions are reviewed?

Build separate timelines for the spinal and airway problems. Include the policy start, applicable waiting-period end, earliest documented signs, diagnostic visits and treatment dates. Use dates actually present in your records and policy documents.

Embrace's pre-existing-condition explanation includes symptoms as well as diagnoses before coverage or during waiting periods, and says new or unrelated conditions may still be covered. This is an insurer-specific example. A recent formal diagnosis does not erase earlier recorded signs, and an excluded condition does not tell you how every other line of a mixed bill will be handled.

Retrieve the old puppy records, ordinary checkups and referral notes. That familiar folder may now help establish what was recorded and when. If an old note is inaccurate, request a dated clinical clarification that preserves the original record. Avoid making your own retrospective diagnosis from a word such as “snoring” or “limping.”

Where should supporting records go, and what if a charge is denied?

Follow the insurer's current upload instructions and retain the submission confirmation. Embrace's claims page distinguishes the invoice submission from supporting documents: chart notes and discharge summaries go through its separate document-upload section. It also describes an Explanation of Benefits and a veterinarian-supported appeal route. Follow your own carrier's procedures and deadlines.

Compare the decision with each invoice line. Ask which diagnosis was assigned to a disputed service, which policy provision was applied and whether a shared charge needs clinical clarification. If you challenge a decision, organize the response around the stated reason and ask the veterinarian for relevant factual support. Keep correspondence and corrected documents together under the claim reference.

While sorting paperwork, keep medical urgency separate. Cornell's BOAS guidance says respiratory crisis requires immediate veterinary or emergency-hospital care. Ask your veterinarian promptly about new neurologic signs; do not delay urgent treatment to finish a claim packet.

Frequently asked questions about French Bulldog insurance claims

Can I submit one invoice for IVDD and BOAS surgery?

Keep the complete invoice and identify both diagnoses. Ask your insurer whether its portal needs one submission or separate linked claims; avoid submitting the same charge twice.

Should shared anesthesia be split equally?

Do not assume an equal split. Request a hospital explanation and the insurer’s allocation instructions. If the charge cannot be reliably separated, label it shared.

Is a credit-card receipt enough?

A payment receipt does not itemize treatment. Embrace requires an itemized invoice showing every charge; check your own insurer’s required documents.

Does a new BOAS diagnosis prove it was not pre-existing?

No. Earlier symptoms may matter under policy terms. Preserve the full history and ask the insurer which records and provisions support its decision.

What should I request after a partial denial?

Request the written reason for each disputed charge, its assigned condition and the relevant policy provision. Follow your insurer’s appeal process and ask the veterinarian for factual clarification.

The best record for French Bulldog insurance claims is a clear one: preserve the complete bill, separate the diagnoses, explain shared charges and keep the original history. Think of it as adding a carefully labeled chapter to that first puppy folder, so the next reviewer can follow the story without guessing.
