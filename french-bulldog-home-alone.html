How Long Can a French Bulldog Stay Home Alone? 79 Owner Answers, Workday Routines and What Each Fix Costs

How long you can leave a French Bulldog home alone is really two questions: how long your dog copes well, and what it costs you to shorten the day when it doesn't. We logged 79 owner answers from an August 2026 Frenchie thread and lined them up against welfare-charity guidance, so you can see where your workday sits before you spend a dollar on cover.

Quick answer: The UK vet charity PDSA advises that most dogs shouldn't be left for much longer than four hours. Real owners stretch further: the median maximum in our sample was 6 hours, 59% kept it at 6 hours or less, and 18% regularly left their Frenchie for 9 hours or more, usually with a second dog, a camera or a midday visit. Plan around 4 to 6 hours for one adult Frenchie on its own, and budget a break for anything longer.

How long do real owners leave their French Bulldog alone?

Most owners keep it to a working morning or less: the median maximum was 6 hours. About a quarter almost never leave their Frenchie alone, while roughly one in six owners regularly goes 9 hours or more.

The source is an August 2026 r/Frenchbulldogs thread, "How long do you leave your Frenchie home alone?", with 165 comments. We kept every top-level answer that gave a number of hours (or "never") for an adult dog, used the top of any range ("4–6 hours" counts as 6), and left out puppies, jokes and dogs described with a medical condition. That gave 79 answers.

The dog decides more than the clock

Two owners in the same band can have very different experiences. One owner described a "perfectly potty trained" Frenchie who still pees in the crate out of anxiety if left for longer than 4 to 5 hours. Another tried 10 hours once, found their dog trembling on their return, and never did it again. At the other end, an owner who leaves for about 9 hours said their camera showed the dogs "literally just slept all day."

What do welfare charities and researchers recommend?

The PDSA's vet Q&A on leaving dogs safely says that "as a rule, most dogs shouldn't be left for much longer than four hours," because by then they may be hungry or need a toilet break. It adds that the right length depends on your dog's personality, routine and home.

The same page recommends building alone time up gradually once your dog is comfortable with short absences, and using a pet camera or monitoring app to check that your dog is relaxed, with someone able to get back quickly if not.

What a large UK study found about real dogs

A 2022 study in the journal Animals, "Impact of Changes in Time Left Alone on Separation-Related Behaviour in UK Pet Dogs", followed 1,807 adult dogs through 2020. Before the first lockdown, in February 2020, 46.2% of the dogs were left alone on 5 to 7 days a week, 43.0% had a longest absence of 3 to 6 hours and 9.3% were regularly left for more than 6 hours.

The finding that matters most for workers going back to the office came later. Dogs whose time alone fell the most during lockdown were at higher risk of developing new signs of separation-related behavior by the October 2020 follow-up, when owners were going out more again (the share of dogs left alone on 5 to 7 days a week had climbed back from 8.7% to 23.0%). By then, 9.9% of dogs that had shown no signs in February had developed at least one. In plain terms, a sudden jump from "never alone" to "alone all day" is the risky part, not just the number of hours.

What does a workable Frenchie workday look like?

Most owners who make full-time work fit don't leave one unbroken block of time. They split the day with a lunch-break trip home, a walker, a relative or daycare, and they put a walk or play session at both ends.

What does each option really cost you?

The cheapest fix is almost always time or a favor: a lunch trip home, a partner's shift or a trusted neighbor. Paid help is best saved for the gap your dog genuinely can't cover, because a daily fee adds up fast over a year.

The full-day sitter in the table works out to roughly $37 per weekday if you spread $800 across about 21.7 weekdays a month, and close to $9,600 a year. That buys a Frenchie who is never alone, but it's the top of the range. A single midday visit only covers the middle of the day, so a dog that is fine for 5 hours and a 9.5-hour workday need just one visit, not a full day of care.

Step by step: moving your Frenchie from remote work to office days

The UK study above is the reason to start weeks ahead, not the night before. Here is a plan built from the PDSA guidance and what owners in the return-to-office thread found worked.

  1. Start four or more weeks out. Practice short absences while you're still working from home, then build them up gradually, as the PDSA recommends.
  2. Rehearse the real routine. Do your office-day morning on a home day: same wake-up, same walk, same leaving signals, then go out for an hour or two.
  3. Tire your dog out first. One owner gives their Frenchie a 30-minute walk before leaving and another as soon as they get home on long days, and says the dog then sleeps while they're out.
  4. Fix the potty math. Do a last trip right before you go. Then check your stretch against the 4–6 hours most owners in our French Bulldog potty break guide keep between daytime trips.
  5. Set up a camera. Several owners only relaxed once they could see their dog napping. If you see crying or pacing instead, you've found your real limit.
  6. Book cover for the gap. Use the planner to see how many midday breaks you need, then line up a walker, neighbor or relative before day one.
  7. Keep the home safe and cool. One owner checks the temperature and runs the AC or a fan before leaving. Flat-faced dogs cope poorly with heat, which our French Bulldog heat guide explains in detail.

How can you tell your Frenchie's limit is shorter than you hoped?

Watch for accidents in a dog that is normally clean, crying or pacing on camera, and a frantic welcome when you come home. Any of these means the stretch is too long or the jump came too fast, so add a midday break and rebuild more slowly.

Owners described all three. One dog peed in its crate after more than 4 to 5 hours despite being fully house-trained. Another cried "and cried and cried" on camera until the owner built the time up slowly. A third came home after 10 hours to a trembling dog. On the other side, owners whose dogs coped well often said the same thing: a calm settle within minutes and long naps on the camera. If the change in behavior is sudden or severe, talk to your vet or a qualified behaviorist rather than just adding hours of training.

Small comforts owners swear by

  • A stuffed food toy, a puzzle toy or a lick mat at the moment you leave.
  • TV, a "dog TV" video or calm music in the background.
  • A piece of clothing that smells of you in their bed or crate.
  • A familiar resting spot, whether an open crate or a bed by the window.

Common mistakes when leaving a French Bulldog alone

  • Going from zero to nine hours overnight. In the UK study, dogs whose time alone dropped the most during lockdown were the ones most at risk of new separation-related signs later that year.
  • Using other owners' hours as permission. "Mine sleeps all day" is true for that dog. Your camera tells you about yours.
  • Paying for more cover than you need. If your dog is fine for 5 hours, a 9-hour day needs one midday visit, not full daycare.
  • Paying for less than you need. A crate accident or a trembling dog costs more in the end than a midday walker.
  • Forgetting the temperature. A warm apartment with the AC off is a real risk for this breed.
  • Having no backup. Walkers cancel and relatives travel. Know who covers on those days before they happen.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours can a French Bulldog be left alone?

The PDSA advises that most dogs shouldn't be left for much longer than four hours. Among 79 Frenchie owners we counted, the median maximum was 6 hours, and owners who went 9 hours or more usually had a second dog, a camera or a midday visit.

Can I own a French Bulldog if I work 9 to 5?

Many owners do, but almost all split the day. A lunch trip home, a midday walker, a relative or daycare keeps the longest stretch near 4 to 6 hours. Plan and price that break before you bring a dog home.

Is it OK to leave a Frenchie alone for 8 hours?

Some adult Frenchies in our sample did it calmly, mostly with company from another pet and a camera to check on them. Others showed crate accidents or distress well before 8 hours. Treat 8 hours as an upper limit for a dog you've seen relaxed on camera, not a starting point.

Will getting a second dog solve it?

It can help a dog that gets bored on its own, and many long-hours owners had two. It won't fix a dog that is distressed about its people leaving, and it doubles your costs.

How do I prepare my Frenchie for going back to the office?

Start weeks ahead with short absences, rehearse your office-day routine, walk your dog before leaving, set up a camera and book midday cover for any gap longer than your dog's limit.

Should I crate my Frenchie while I'm at work?

Owners were split. Some used a crate their dog treats as a safe spot, others gave the run of one or two rooms. Whichever you choose, it has to be somewhere your dog already relaxes, and the length of the stretch still matters.

Spend on the gap, not the whole day

So how long can you leave a French Bulldog home alone? Welfare guidance says about four hours, and most owners stay at or under six. The smart investment is a dog trained to settle, then paid or borrowed help for exactly the stretch it can't cover.
